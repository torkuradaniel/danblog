Over the last twelve months, the African tech scene has experienced a surge in infrastructure startups. This article looks into this trend and why it is good for the ecosystem. But, to be fair, this article is about me and my love for infrastructure startups.

While there had been significant ecosystem activity before then, the launch of **Paystack** and **Flutterwave** (both infra startups) can be said to be the catalyst of what I refer to as *Naija Tech Age 2.0*. While the infra they built powered digital payments, the relatively newer infra companies are tackling a broad range of problems and providing the launchpad for other startups and developers to offer better services unrestrained.

---

## The Playbook 📕

Let me start with a snippet from *The Everything Store*. After all, “the everything store” popularised this whole infra thing when they launched **AWS in 2004**:

> “If Amazon wanted to stimulate creativity among its developers, it shouldn't try to guess what kind of services they might want; … Instead, it should be creating primitives — the building blocks of computing — and then get out of the way.”

Amazon realised early on that a company (the software) comprises multiple components. These components come together to form software that provides value to users.

For example, a loan app (Fintech) like **Carbon** has different parts:
- Registration (OTP and related services)  
- KYC  
- Loan decisioning (whether to give out a loan or not)  
- Fraud handling  
- Cash in / Cash out (CICO)  
- Servers that host the code  

Amazon also found out that not all companies have or need to build the infra they require. Reasons include **time, cost, and talent**. By using infra created by other companies, startups avoid:

- **Building from scratch** → saves talent and resources  
- **Maintenance & enhancements** → handled by infra providers  
- **Edge cases** → tricky scenarios solved once by infra providers  
- **Regulation & licensing** → plug into compliant infra like Flutterwave or Stitch  

---

## Boom Boom 💥💥

It’s no coincidence that Nigeria’s current tech boom started with **Paystack** and **Flutterwave**, both payment infra startups. Payment is at the core of any digital economy.

Since then, we’ve seen a surge in startups leveraging infra to build amazing products. Some hot infra startups that raised in the past year include:

### Mono / Stitch / Okra
- Provide secure open banking infrastructure for access to financial data  
- Enable lending apps, financial management apps, and direct bank payments  

### Pngme
- Democratizes access to credit history data  
- Offers ML models and data infra for better lending decisions  

### Union54
- API to issue debit cards without needing a bank or third-party processor  

### Bloc
- End-to-end service: payments, card issuance APIs, and more  

Other exciting infra startups: **Klasha, OnePipe, Okra, Indicina**

---

## Textbook Move 📚

Some African companies are now opening up their internal infra for others to use:
- **Gokada** → delivery APIs with riders  
- **BuyCoins & Quidax** → trading APIs  
- **Helium Health** → health infra  

---

## Brass Needs Brass and Asphalt

Brass underscores the importance of infra: the ability to build and launch fast. To deliver branchless business banking, Brass leverages a suite of services for seamless UX.

---

## What This Means

### Launch and Test Faster
Startups can launch competitive offerings quickly, test with users, and iterate.

### Innovation
Focus on unique value propositions instead of reinventing infra.

### Improved UX
With fewer infra headaches, startups can delight users with better experiences.

---

## Conclusion

A side effect of product being a commodity is that businesses must become more innovative. Better infra stops being a differentiator, so startups must think deeply about moats and customer acquisition.
