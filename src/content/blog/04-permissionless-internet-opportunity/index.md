# No Permission Required

*By Daniel Torkura*

> “The internet has become an institution of its own that’s reshaping who can become an expert, how they share their expertise and the ways they can earn a living from it.” — Fadeke Adegbuyi

---

## A Friend’s Clothing Store

A few weeks ago, my friend launched a clothing brand almost overnight. She set up her store and was ready to sell the same night. In 2015, this would have been unthinkable — renting a shop, stocking inventory, and hoping customers showed up. Today, thanks to the internet, she was ready in under 24 hours.

---

## Pre-2016: Permission Required

### Credentialing
- No credentials, no access.  
- Gatekeepers controlled entry into specialised fields.  
- People sought credentials, others sold them, and institutions enforced them.

### Networks and Circles
> “Expertise remains crucial to knowledge production, but it must reflect genuine excellence in a field, rather than mere affiliation with institutional authority.” — Jacob Mchangama

Access was restricted to those with pedigree or institutional recognition. Elite institutions like Harvard offered both credentials and networks, often bringing capital.

### Capital
Capital was king. Upfront fixed costs made starting nearly impossible for many. Lack of access to capital kept people from trying new ideas.

### Gatekeepers
> “The internet is a democratising force — fewer gatekeepers, more participants.” — Matt Clifford

Preconceived notions of expertise meant only certain people were acknowledged. Others played status games to fit in.

---

## Post-2016: Permissionless

Technology democratised access to information, tools, and distribution. Expertise, capital, and networks became more open.

### Education
Self-taught software engineers without degrees now work at top startups. Proof of work matters more than credentials.

### Entertainment
Entertainers rise via YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram. Viewers decide, not institutions. Nollywood casting is influenced by online popularity.

### Starting a Business
Indie restaurants launch on WhatsApp and Instagram with minimal capital. Platforms like **Pledre** enable entrepreneurs to start schools online with near-zero costs.

---

## Still Early Days

We’ve built great tools, but more needs to be done to make starting truly permissionless.

### Starting Should Be Easier
Lower barriers mean more participants. Tools like **riverside.fm** and **YouTube** reduce hurdles for creators. Infrastructure acts like capital — AWS enabled startups to launch with near-zero upfront costs.

### Making Money Should Be Easier
Creators struggle to earn sustainably. Innovative business models are needed.  
- **Strike** uses crypto for global payments.  
- **Heyfood** helps indie restaurants manage businesses and payments seamlessly.

---

## Conclusion

The internet has opened enormous opportunities. We must build tools and infrastructure that allow young Africans to take creative risks permissionlessly. They already do so much with so little — imagine what happens if we get out of their way.

> “We can be so much more. I believe this so deeply it hurts.” — Emmanuel Quartey
