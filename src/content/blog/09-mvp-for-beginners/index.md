# MVP for Beginners

*By Daniel Torkura*

> **TL; DR**: An MVP is a litmus test for demand validation and learning.

MVP stands for **Minimum Viable Product** (or Minimum Valuable Product). You’ve probably heard the term thrown around as a buzzword. The goal here is to leave you not only with a good grip of what an MVP is but also capable of applying it.

---

## What an MVP Is Not

- ❌ Not the least appealing version of your product.  
- ❌ Not necessarily the first version of your product.  

Examples of popular MVPs:  
- Facebook  
- YouTube  
- Amazon  
- Twitter  

These products started simple but validated demand before expanding features.

---

## Building a Product Is Science

**Idea → Hypothesis → Experiment → Proof → Reality**

An MVP is how you test your hypothesis:  
- Do users care about this problem?  
- Will they pay for it?  
- Does it reduce uncertainty and increase confidence?  

Example: **Amazon** started by selling books online to validate digital commerce.

---

## MVP to the Rescue

An MVP helps you:  
- Test assumptions quickly.  
- Reduce wasted time, money, and effort.  
- Validate demand before scaling.  

---

## PMF (Product-Market Fit)

An MVP is a test for PMF.  
- PMF means the market has shown enough buy-in.  
- A product with PMF becomes integral to users’ lives.  

---

## Building an MVP

### Understand the Problem
- Avoid assuming you are the user.  
- Talk to prospective users early.  
- Use personas and “day in the life” tools.  

### Think Alternatives, Not Competitors
- Users often rely on alternatives (e.g., pen and paper vs. to-do apps).  
- Use the **Jobs To Be Done** framework to uncover insights.  

### Prioritization Is Key
- Focus on what is truly “minimum.”  
- Use the **Kano Model** to prioritize features:  
  - Basic (non-negotiable)  
  - Performance (proportional delight)  
  - Delighters (unexpected delight, e.g., Snapchat stories)  

---

## Minimum Viable Problem

Define your value proposition, pick a small subset, and solve for them.  
Example: A one-page website builder focusing first on white-collar professionals.

---

## Why Building an MVP Isn’t Sexy

- **Solution Bias** → Building a key without knowing the door.  
- **Big Product Vision** → Temptation to skip MVP.  
- **Myth of Sophistication** → More features ≠ more value.  
- **Blind Optimism** → Hope without validation.  

---

## Time to Learn

Once your MVP is ready:  
- Talk to customers (focus on problems, not feature requests).  
- Create testable narratives (e.g., “sign-up must take 6 seconds”).  
- Turn data into insights (understand the *why* behind metrics).  
- Avoid vanity metrics — focus on actionable ones.  

---

## Conclusion

An MVP is not about building something small and ugly. It’s about **learning fast, validating demand, and reducing uncertainty**. Done right, it sets the foundation for achieving Product-Market Fit and scaling with confidence.
