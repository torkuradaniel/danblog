Most people overcomplicate things when trying to explain what growth is. Growth is the entire process of how you take a prospective user from being aware of your product to becoming an evangelist, using a data-driven approach.

Growth can get complicated quickly if it isn’t measured (and visualized) to know what strategy is working. Funnels play a huge part in understanding growth. Thinking in terms of funnels helps measure growth and gives an in-depth view of how well your product is performing.

---

## Funnel Thinking

Funnels are literal: imagine how a substance passes through a funnel from top to bottom. That’s how customers ideally move — from awareness to conversion.

Growth happens via **channels** and is understood using **funnels**. Channels answer the question: *How will you get users?*

---

## Popular Growth Funnels

### AARRR Framework (Pirates Funnel)
- **Acquisition** → How users find you  
- **Activation** → First experience or trial  
- **Retention** → Users stick around  
- **Revenue** → Paying customers  
- **Referral** → Users bring others  

Example: A social media tool acquires via SEO, activates with a free trial, retains users who stay, earns revenue when they pay, and grows via referrals.

### Julian’s Growth Funnel
- **Acquisition → Conversion → Engagement → Revenue → Referral**  
Focuses on engagement instead of retention, highlighting the user-product relationship.

### E-commerce Growth Funnel
- **Acquisition → Revenue → Referral → Retention**  
Tailored for online shopping platforms like Jumia or Amazon.

---

## Earlier Funnel Models

- **AIDAOR (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action → Onboarding → Retention)**  
- **ACCA / AISDALSLove** (Awareness → Comprehension → Conviction → Action, etc.)  

These early models were more qualitative and harder to measure with quantitative metrics.

---

## Measuring Growth

Funnels are useless if you aren’t measuring. Key methods include:
- **A/B Testing**  
- **Cohort Analysis**  
- **Analytics** (Product, Marketing, Behavioral)

---

## Case Studies

### Nigerian Prince Scam
- Acquisition: Few people enter the funnel  
- Retention: High (those who fall, stay)  
- Revenue: High conversion rate despite small volume  

### Showmax Example
- Acquisition: Twitter Ads  
- Activation: Free trial  
- Retention: User stays after trial  
- Revenue: User pays  
- Referral: User tells friends  

Comparison: The Nigerian Prince achieves higher efficiency despite fewer users.

---

## Key Takeaway

Real growth comes from the **bottom of the funnel**, not the top. Vanity metrics (page views, signups) can be misleading. What matters is efficiency and how strategies impact retention, revenue, and referrals.
